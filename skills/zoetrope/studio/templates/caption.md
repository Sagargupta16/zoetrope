# Caption rules (the text under the film, `post.json`)

- The first line is one sentence under 125 characters. It makes the claim and does not give the payoff away.
- The second paragraph is the AI disclosure, not the last: the pictures and the voice are AI-generated, the
  pictures are illustrations and not photographs of the real things or places, and diagrams and cards are drawn
  or redrawn by us.
- Then the facts in plain sentences, each source's own qualifier kept ("about", "reportedly", "one study").
  Where a fact rests on one source, say so.
- A negative is written as what we found, never as a universal: "we found no published test", not "nobody has
  published one".
- What the script moved out of the voice ("For the caption" in script.md) goes here.
- A "Sources:" line names each source by its kind (a letter, a review, a dictionary, a journal paper).
- A closing question.
- At most 2,000 characters in the caption and 3 to 5 hashtags (`hashtags` is optional; leave it out when the
  film goes somewhere that does not use them).
- `alt_text` describes the film for someone who cannot see it, numbers included.
- `sources` is a list of `{title, url}` taken from facts.md rows; every url is one a row cites. Whoever posts the film may publish this list with the caption, so it names nothing the caption could not say (no person the film leaves unnamed, in a title or in a url).
- `cover_ms` is a moment where the hook has settled and no caption page is up. Look at that frame.
- No em or en dashes, no emoji.
