## House rules that already cost time

- The spoken hook is eight words or fewer with the surprise in its first four words; its speech is over by 3 s.
  The first six seconds also say what the thing is.
- Two columns in the script: `caption` (digits) and `spoken` (numbers as words, no abbreviations, nothing the
  voice misreads). Names are a risk for the voice: list the ones to test in a real read and respell the
  spoken form where it comes out wrong: {names}.
- 27 to 32 lines, 285 to 315 spoken words, each line 18 words or fewer, one idea per line; a fresh reason to
  stay about every 15 to 20 s; a payoff at about 70 to 85 percent; the last line answers the first.
- A question the film leaves open is asked on screen early and answered late, and the stretch between carries it.
- True as worded, with the sources' qualifiers; our own arithmetic is labelled as ours or left out. A figure
  with one source keeps its qualifier and the caption says it has one source.
- On-screen text never repeats the caption word for word; a chip adds a place, a date or a source. Label chips
  are dark, date chips yellow. A source line on a generated picture says what it is ("Fact from: ...").
- No em or en dashes, no emoji, no semicolons in spoken text, no greeting, no "did you know", no "in this video".
