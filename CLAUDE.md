## What this is

This repo is the public `zoetrope` Claude Code plugin (`/plugin marketplace add Sagargupta16/zoetrope`, then `/plugin install zoetrope@zoetrope`). The skill lives in `skills/zoetrope/` (SKILL.md, `engine/`, `references/`, `kits/`, `setup.sh`), the agents in `agents/`, the manifests in `.claude-plugin/`. Locally, `~/.claude/skills/zoetrope` is a junction to `skills/zoetrope/` and `~/.claude/agents/zoetrope` a junction to `agents/`, so an edit here is live in the next session that loads the skill.

## Repo rules

- **One name, one repo.** The skill, the plugin, the marketplace, the folder and the repo are all `zoetrope`; never a copy under another name. Skill text names no user's home folder: `<skill>` (the skill folder as an absolute path, the base directory shown when the skill loads) or `~/.cache/zoetrope` instead, and `scripts/check.py` fails on a home path (ZC004).
- **Keep the scrub clean.** The original platform's tool names, portal link, team, author and chat channel were rewritten out on 2026-10-28 and the history was squashed. Do not add them back. Before every push, run the identifier grep kept in `~/.claude/projects/c--Code-GitHub/memory/zoetrope-internal-skill.md`; the only hits allowed are the English word "quick".
- **Keep it general** (owner, 2026-11-20). zoetrope is a general video tool, used for more than one kind of video. Guidance made for one account or platform (its hooks, captions, end card, posting queue) lives with that project, and no card, reference or engine file names an account. An engine feature a project needed (photo plates, ambience beds, voice polish) belongs here when any video can use it.
- Every change gets a CHANGELOG line under a concrete version, and README wherever it describes the changed part. A release bumps `version` in `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` and `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` to the CHANGELOG head (ZC007).
- **The README diagram** `docs/zoetrope-flow.svg` is exported from `docs/zoetrope-flow.workflow.json` with the archify skill. When the flow changes, edit the JSON and set `meta.repository.revision` to the new commit, run archify `finalize workflow` with `--repo-root .`, open the HTML in a Chrome DevTools page (the browser pane cannot open `file://`), run `Archify.exportMenu.run('svg')` while catching the blob from `URL.createObjectURL`, replace its two non-ASCII separators (U+203A, U+00B7) with `&gt;` and `|`, and look at it at 880 px in both themes.
- Files are LF (`.gitattributes` sets `eol=lf`), unlike most Windows repos in this workspace. Check the bytes before assuming either way.
- Everything a build makes goes in `outputs/` (gitignored): videos, working folders, scratch scripts, history bundles. The one exception is the README's promo in `docs/` (`.gitignore` lets `docs/*.mp4` and `docs/*.gif` through).

## Engine gotchas (hit on 2026-10-28 and 2026-10-29)

- **Windows workers.** `run_all.py` work sits under `if __name__ == "__main__":`, and `render.py` imports `scenes` inside its functions, because each worker must set `E.W`, `E.H` before `scenes` reads them. A top-level import rendered a 9:16 piece at 16:9 size.
- **Fonts.** The cards name Mac fonts. `engine.py` lists Windows Arial first and raises on a missing font; before that, Pillow silently fell back to its 10 px default and every headline came out tiny. The 14 OFL families 24 cards name are cached by `fonts.py` (2.2.4) and drawn by name. A variable file is always set to the weight asked for, because their default instances differ: Nunito's is 200, Cormorant's 300, Fraunces' 900.
- **OpenBLAS threads.** `contract.py` sets `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` before numpy loads. Unset, one process that loaded numpy and ran a matrix product held 27 threads and 793 MB private, against 4 threads and 54 MB at 1; seven reel builds of eight workers ran out of memory on 2026-10-30.
- **Sleep.** The app holds the PC awake only while a session works, and on battery only with Keep awake on battery power on (turned on 2026-10-31). On 2026-10-29 the laptop went on battery at 07:19, idle-slept at 07:29 during the final review and woke only when plugged in at 13:59: 6.5 of the build's 8.4 hours. A closed lid sleeps whatever the setting.
- **9:16 text.** Size titles with `fit_size` against the short side (`min(W, H)`), or they overflow the safe area (Q201).
- **True peak.** The AAC encode adds true peak: up to 1.23 dB at 192k, at most 0.45 dB at 320k (9 mixes). So `audio.master` limits to -2.0 dBTP with a numpy 4x true-peak limiter, the mux encodes AAC 320k, and `run_all.py` re-masters lower when the decoded MP4 reads over -1.3. When Q802 still fails it names every sound present at that moment; lower the first one in its cut order.
- **Cue sync (Q902).** Q902 measures each cue's own sound on the lossless SFX stem from the cue sheet, not by guessing onsets in the mix. The 37 to 87 ms lateness of the first batch was placement: slow attacks placed by their first sample (a 0.25 s pad attack is heard 44 ms in). The mux adds 0.000 ms; the old alimiter added 4.0 ms. Place sounds with `synth.place` by their landmark.
- **Sample rates.** Every audio module runs at 44.1 kHz; only `audio.master` writes 48 kHz. Mixing 48 kHz voice sample by sample into a 44.1 kHz bed drifts the timing.
- **File size.** Film grain drives size: grain std 2.6 gave 140.2 MB for 60 s, std 1.6 at CRF 20 gave 35.9 MB. Q1002 notes bitrate over 12 Mb/s.
- **Parallel renders.** A raw 1080p frame is 6.2 MB. Sent back from render workers through a process pipe while five builds of 5 processes shared the machine, 7 of 45 renders died with WinError 1450 (2 of 6 at three builds of 8). `render.py` passes frames through a shared-memory ring instead: 0 of 40 and 0 of 6, byte-identical output (2026-10-29).
- **Console encoding.** A redirected Windows console is cp1252: printing a caption with an arrow crashed a passing build. Rows print ASCII (`contract.line`).
- **stdin.** An ffmpeg decode without `-nostdin` stops early with exit 0 when a key is waiting (a 60 s check read 200 of 1800 frames). Every decode closes stdin and checks its frame count.
- **str hash.** `hash("text")` changes in every process (PYTHONHASHSEED). A seed made from it gave each render worker different lightning in the g29 kit; seed from `zlib.crc32` or `engine.rng`.
- **Durations as quotients.** `5/30` is not exactly 5 frames as a float; `compile_schedule` reads floats as fractions with a denominator up to 10**6.
- **Windows locks.** A folder that is a process cwd or a watched folder cannot be moved or deleted. Move its contents instead, and keep shells out of `outputs/` subfolders.

## Lessons absorbed on 2026-11-12 (from the clean-room public version, written from this engine on 2026-11-01)

- **Refuse before you clean.** A stage that refuses (no ffmpeg, a stale picture for `audio`, a size budget with no video bitrate) must do it before `clean()` and before `build.json` is written. `audio` used to check `picture_is_current` after deleting `out.mp4` and the mixes, so a refused run with the wrong `--size` destroyed the last good build and rewrote `build.json`.
- **A cut file gets a verdict, not a traceback.** A copy cut to 35 to 50 percent of its bytes still has every frame counted by ffprobe (the moov box is in front), but its last frame does not decode: Q502 stopped `qa.py check` with `ValueError: cannot reshape array of size 0`. Q501 and Q502 now read NOT CHECKED there, like Q503, and `files.cut` plants it.
- **No ffmpeg means stop.** Every rung of the encode ladder needs ffmpeg (the master measures loudness with it too); only a build without libx264 drops to rung b or c.
- **`cp -r engine/*` drops the Python pin.** A glob skips the hidden `.python-version`, silently; copy with `engine/.`. Without the pin a proof and its render can land on different interpreters, and Q1304 sees the change.
- **Docs drift from code.** Rewriting the references from the code found docs that disagreed with the engine, among them the template bed's docstring (no bass or pad line: a tick and a tone), `render.CRF` (only `run_all.CRF` changes the build), a `qa_sync` comment (PAD_TOL, not 0.04 s), and a lint pointer to a table that did not exist. Every number in a reference names its constant or is dated to a run.

## 3D plates and recorded instruments (hit on 2026-11-10)

- **This folder is live while you edit it.** The junction makes every save the skill. On 2026-11-10 another session was building a reels batch from here while 2.4.0 was written: keep `engine/` runnable at every save, and add a card, its kit and its router row in one step.
- **Blender's view transform.** Its default (AgX) turned the card's palette pastel; `stage3d.py` uses Khronos PBR Neutral, which keeps hue and saturation and rolls the highlights off. Standard clipped a lit cream floor to flat white.
- **Paths into Blender.** A relative `render.filepath` is read from the drive root: the first test wrote four frames to `C:\plates`. `stage3d.run` resolves `--out`.
- **bpy first.** `bmesh`, `mathutils` and `bpy_extras` exist only after `import bpy`; an import sorter puts `bmesh` first and the stage dies. The two lines carry `# isort: skip`.
- **Samples.** 48, 64 and 160 paths a pixel look the same on a character close-up after the denoiser, at 2.6, 3.3 and 8.1 s a 1080p frame (RTX 4070 Laptop, OptiX): final quality is 64.
- **uv keeps no wheel an offline run can find.** `uv run --offline --with bpy==5.2.3` fails right after an online run that worked (uv 0.13.6), so `plate3d.missing()` reads a note that `fetch()` leaves in `~/.cache/zoetrope/blender/`, not the cache. Since 2026-11-16 the note is named for the lock (`bpy-5.2.4-<12 hex of the lock sha256>.txt`), so a new lock asks for a fetch again.
- **uv run does not check `--hash` lines.** `uv run --with-requirements` installed a package whose hash in the file was wrong (uv 0.13.8, 2026-11-16; neither a `--require-hashes` line in the file nor `[pip] require-hashes` in uv.toml changed that, and `uv run` reads no pylock.toml), while `uv pip install --require-hashes` refuses it, a cached wheel too. So `envs.py` (2.7.2) builds every environment that way: a working folder's `.venv`, which `uv run --no-project --with-requirements requirements.txt` finds and uses with nothing added once every pin is met, and each side environment in `~/.cache/zoetrope/envs/<lock>-py<ver>-<sha12>/`. A `uv run` with no `.venv` in its folder or a parent still gets an unchecked overlay (the storyboard's `qa.py holds` from `<skill>/engine`). A venv built in a temp folder and renamed works (`uv venv --relocatable`); a `.venv` cannot replace itself on Windows while it runs `envs.py`.
- **A cleaned uv cache.** Something outside the session cleaned uv's cache mid-build (`C:` went from 82 to 426 GB free): for a minute `import numpy` failed in the engine environment from a half-removed entry, and Blender (324 MB) downloaded again on the next render. Retry before debugging.
- **The lint follows imports.** Q1202 reads every local module `scenes.py` imports. `plate3d.py` timed its progress lines with `time.perf_counter()` and the proof failed on it: that file reads no clock.
- **Sample tuning.** `sampler.py`'s manifest holds each recording's measured root and cents (FFT peak, median over the loud frames): most VSCO 2 CE and VCSL file names put middle C at C3 (the harp's at C4), the glockenspiel is 8 to 25 cents sharp and four of the five timpani sit near quarter tones. Measure a new row the same way; autocorrelation cannot read the mallets or timpani (inharmonic). A second reader with a first-period autocorrelation put harp, strings, flute and horn within 3.1 cents.
- **Contact is placed from the rig and measured, not worked out.** The pilot's star was placed by arithmetic twice and two reviews each found a P0 at the catch (the head through the star, then the hand inside it for seven frames) while every gate passed. It now hangs at `S3.where(hand) + HOLD`, read from the rig posed as at the catch, and `python plate3d.py run stage.py contact` prints the hand-to-star gap per frame with no render. Do both for anything that is caught, held or handed over.
- **A re-render is not byte-identical.** The same frame rendered twice differed by 0.11 of 255 on average and 9 at most (GPU path tracing and the denoiser). Never render an approved plate again unless its stage changed: every frame would read as changed (Q1301).
- **Heredocs.** The Bash tool turned `"\n"` inside a Python heredoc into a real line break and broke `run_all.py` for a minute. Patch files that hold a backslash with the Edit tool.

## Studio engine gotchas (hit on 2026-11-24 and 2026-11-25)

The second engine, `skills/zoetrope/studio/` (manual: `skills/zoetrope/references/studio.md`, rules: `studio-checklist.md`). The skill description sends web UI animation and voiceless SVG or HTML clips to ui-motion and keeps narrated HTML-stage films here; keep both halves when the description changes.

- **A shell heredoc over about 8 kB is cut off.** A `shots.json` or `graphics.js` written that way fails with an unmatched quote. Write long files with the file tool.
- **ComfyUI answers a repeated still from its cache.** An identical request (same prompt, seed and settings) writes no new file, so the newest file under a prefix can be an older picture. `gen.py` asks the history for the files of its own request; to get a different picture change the seed (`--only <id> --reseed`) or the prompt.
- **At most two clips in a chain.** A shot with `from` starts on the last frame of the shot it names; a third link in a row is a rule break, not a tuning choice, and `studio.py clips` refuses it (exit 3) before any clip is made.
- **`--only` reached `gen.py` as a list.** `studio.py stills <build> --only s03` crashed (`'list' object has no attribute 'split'`) while the same command without `--only` passed, so a proof that never names a shot proves nothing about remakes. `gen.shot_ids()` takes both forms, and `gen.py selftest` runs in the quick selftest.
- **Clips are 30 fps.** `gen.py` makes them at 30 and writes that to `media/state.json`; `build.py` reads the rate from there and assumes 24 when the state has none, so a clip made outside `gen.py` plays at the wrong speed until its state is written.
- **A kit change moves pixels only in new batches.** `studio.py new-batch` copies the whole `studio/` folder, so a running film keeps the kit it started on and is never switched mid-film. `studio.py diff <batch>` lists what changed, went missing or was added in `studio/`, `engine/` and `docs/` since the batch's `SNAPSHOT.txt`; port a change deliberately and compare frames before and after.
- **Template folders are named `readers/`.** `.gitignore` drops `review/` and `frames/` anywhere, and `scripts/check.py` skips them.
- **A film's own `graphics.js` loads after the kit.** A device it defines under a kit device's name replaces the kit's for that film; a built-in overlay type in `kit.js` wins over both, so promote a new device into `kit_graphics.js`, never into `TYPES`.
- **Chrome's frames are not bit-stable.** Two runs of the same code differed by 1 or 2 of 255 on 3 to 58 edge pixels of large type (the GPU rasteriser, measured 2026-11-25 on the probe and on a finished film). `selftest.py --render` compares a frame by its digest first, then by the mean colour of 60 px blocks; a one-level difference on type edges is not a kit change.
- **The studio and the audio engine share module names** (`qa.py`, `audio.py`, `render.py`). Loaded in one process, `import qa` resolved to the wrong file after the mix. `studio.py` runs narration, the mix and the file check as their own processes; keep it that way.
- **`studio.py` starts its own environment.** `voice`, `render`, `sheet` and every check but `plan`, `lint` and `stills` run again through `uv run --no-project --with-requirements studio/requirements.txt` when playwright, Pillow or numpy is missing. A stage script run on its own (`qa.py`, `render.py`) does not, and reads NOT CHECKED without them.
- **A copied build reads as stale.** `cp -r` gives every file a new time, so a copied film's stills are newer than its `timeline.json` and every check, `render`, `table` and `package` refuse (exit 3, naming the file) until `studio.py build` runs on the copy.
- **No check ends on a `0 FIX` line.** `check all` prints one `check <stage>: exit <n>` line per stage (`plan`, `lint`, `frames`, `lint-frames`, `stills`, `file`), then `check all: exit <n>`, the worst of them; a FIX row that `accept.json` names does not count. A gate that greps for `0 FIX` never matches: `film.js` reads the exit code and the `check all: exit 0` line.
- **A remade still is unanswered again.** `check stills` counts an answer older than its still's `media/<id>.png` as a FIX, and the remake also makes the timeline stale: run `build`, `check frames` (it rewrites `proof/checklist.md`), look at the new frames, then write the six answers again.
- **One list of negators.** The caption pager (`kit.js`) and lint rule L13 (`lint.py`) must hold the same words; when the lint knew more (`don't`, `nothing`), the pager made pages the lint then failed. `selftest.py` compares the two lists.
- **A film its readers passed gets `polish.js`, not another fix round.** Measured 2026-11-25 on three films: a fix round (a sweep of the whole film, then three fresh readers) took 3 to 4 hours and came back READY with a few findings still open, while one builder with a short list of named changes and the owner's ruling on each took 17 to 33 minutes (by hand, before the script existed) and two fresh checkers with a closed list another 24 to 30. Those two checkers found faults that the builder's own frames did not show on two of the three films, so `polish.js` runs one checker per lens after the builder. Keep the fix round for a film with a P0 or a reader that says NOT READY.
- **Workflow scripts had no syntax check.** They run as the body of an async function, so `node --check` on the file itself rejects them; `selftest.py` wraps each one first, and refuses a script that does not open with its `meta` literal or calls a clock or a random number (`Date.now`, `new Date`, a bare `Date(` or `Math.random`, however spaced: those throw in a workflow).
- **A third of all cuts came one frame late.** `build.py` wrote a frame's time rounded to four places, so frame 1214 (40.4666... s) became 40.4667, a hair after the frame, and the renderer shows a shot from the first frame at or after its start. Caption pages start on their own frame, so a new page stood one frame on the old picture (found by a frame-exact check on a film, 2026-11-25). `snap` now cuts the fifth place, so a stored time is never after its frame; the probe's golden timeline changed in two values and its rendered frames still match by digest.
- **The reading-hold check counted one neighbour.** A ten-word source line that stood 7.17 s over four shots was called short at 3.33 s, and a builder weakened its wording to pass. P07 now walks every consecutive shot that carries the same words.
- **The engine's first film came back READY and half empty.** `film.js` told its builders "up to five" clips against the rule list's 8 to 14, and nothing measured a drawing on a bare plate, so 22 s and 17 s of small diagrams on a flat plate passed three readers (2026-11-25). `film.js` now takes `clips` (8 to 12), the plan check has P12 (a bare plate for more than 10 s in a row is a FIX) and P13 (a NOTE under 4 clips a minute), and the rule list says a drawing goes over a blurred, drifting picture and fills its band. All READY means no reader found a fault, not that the film is good: look at the sheets yourself before queueing. Since the second film `studio.py look <build>` makes those sheets from the file (a frame every 1.25 s) and `package` refuses a film whose sheets are older than its video; on that film the look found a curve label standing at the wrong mark and a dark 6 s stretch that three READY readers had listed only as findings.
- **The commands are fast; the agents' thinking is the time.** The first film on the engine took 8.4 hours: voice 33 minutes, build 111, checks 105, prepare 169, gate 10, three readers 73. In the four building agents the commands (build, checks, stills, render) took 59 of 418 minutes; the rest was the model, because no agent named a model or an effort and each ran on the session's model at the session's effort. Every agent of the workflow scripts names both now (`models`, `efforts`): script preparation for the next film took 47 minutes where the first took 173 (draft 60 to 12 minutes, revision 58 to 9), with the same nine agents. Before making a command faster, measure where an agent's minutes went: `agent-*.jsonl` in the workflow's transcript folder holds a timestamp for every tool call and result.
- **A batch's engine copy is upgraded, never swapped by hand.** `python <newer>/studio/studio.py upgrade <batch>` replaces `studio/`, `engine/` and `docs/`, rewrites the snapshot, lists what changed and keeps the old copy in `engine-before-upgrade/`; it refuses (3) a copy that was edited, added to (a font in `studio/fonts/`) or deleted from since its snapshot, and one with no `SNAPSHOT.txt`; `--force` names what it drops. It moves parts by rename only, so a failure (exit 2) leaves the old copy. It once checked only the files the snapshot listed, so an added file vanished into `engine-before-upgrade/` without a word. Run it only when no builder is using the batch, then `build` every open film again.
- **Every motion runs on its shot's own clock.** The camera shake and three jitters ran on film time, so a shot changed its pixels when a gap earlier in the film changed. They run on the shot's or the device's start now, and a film's own graphics must do the same; `changed` relies on it when it compares shot by shot after a cut moved (`keep` saves the timeline beside the film for that). The shake seed also depended on the sum of the id's characters, so `s01` and `s10` shook alike; it now depends on their order, which moves every shaken still once, and `changed` against a film rendered before 2026-11-25 flags those shots. The top 12 px, where the progress bar runs on film time, are left out of that comparison (the word captions run on film time too).
- **`film.js` saved its readers' findings only inside a fix round.** A film that came back all READY had none on disk; `studio.py review save <build> <the workflow's result file> --stage review` now reads the last round that has a reader out of that file; a file with no reader exits 1 and saves nothing, never an empty answers file.
- **A tool that pairs frames by time pairs neighbours when timestamps slip.** `render.py` joined its parts with the concat demuxer, which took each part's length from its container in milliseconds, so every frame after a join ran up to a millisecond early; ffmpeg's psnr filter then compared each frame with its neighbour and `changed` named three untouched shots at 25 dB (film 11, 2026-11-25; by frame number they measured 49 to 54 dB). The join list now gives each part's exact length, `changed` numbers the frames of both films before it compares them, and its selftest holds a film with a planted slip.
- **`look` read a film's length from its container.** ffprobe ends the csv row of a stream that carries side data with a comma (`112.266667,` for a rendered film, whose frames bring an ICC profile), `float()` failed on it and the fallback took the container's length, 0.1 s longer than the picture. So the last cell of every finished film's sheets was empty and nobody saw the last frame (2026-11-26). `sheet.seconds()` reads the row's first field, `look` refuses when ffmpeg writes no frame, and the selftest's film is built like a rendered one (a PNG with an ICC profile under a longer sound): the plain test film had passed.
- **A read-only research lane wrote no file.** `script-prep.js` told its fact lane to write `facts.md`; run as a read-only agent type it returned its rows as text, and the draft, the readers and both traces went on without the file (2026-11-26). Every research lane of `script-prep.js` and `script-revise.js` is followed by a `keep` agent that saves the reply when the file is missing. The same run's last trace said a paper had no release schedule that stood in its Methods: a trace row that says not found now names the words it searched for.
- **`script` dropped a lexicon added by hand.** A respelling the voice needs in every line lived only in `script.json`, and each `script` run rewrote that file without it. It goes in the `## Lexicon` table of `script.md` (`word`, `say`); a `script.md` without the table keeps the earlier file's lexicon, `lead` and `progress`.
- **The caption pager knew dates in one order only.** A film that said "29 February" and "23 people" paged them as 29 / FEBRUARY and 23 / PEOPLE (2026-11-25): the pager glued month then day and a list of units without people or pair. It now keeps a date in either order and those units together. The next film's readers found more of the same kind: a number alone for 0.12 s (THE OTHER / 2), ALMOST on the page before NOBODY TAKES. So a hedge opens the page of its negator, and a lone number joins the page before it after a determiner or at the end of its clause and the page after it when it would only flash. `selftest.py` pins the pages of thirteen sentences. A change to the pager moves every later page of a film, so a finished film keeps the engine it was made with.
- **`status` called every script stale.** The voice step writes its measured read into `script.md` after `script` ran, so the file was always the newer one. The script column now compares the table's lines with `script.json`'s.
- **A fix row of the last fact trace comes back.** A script whose final trace said "fix (minor)" on one line went to the voice unchanged, and the honesty reader of the finished film raised the same words. Changing the line then cost a second pass; before the voice it costs 21 s (the narration is cached per line). Rule on every row of the last trace that is not "holds" before `film.js` starts.
- **A pop is part of the layout.** The `answers` device lit a wide row with a pop to 1.16 from its left edge and ran it past the frame edge for three frames; no check shot a frame inside the pop. The row's peak is capped where its right edge meets the safe box, and `check frames` shoots the frame after an answer row lands. A new device that scales landed type needs the same two things.
- **`studio.py changed` is the regression check for a pass.** It compares two renders frame by frame (ffmpeg's psnr filter) and names the shots that differ. On three films of 2026-11-25 every frame outside the named shots was bit-identical; a sampled look at eight times cannot show that.
- **A carried overlay entered again after every cut.** A hook, a number or a stamp that goes on into the next shot replayed its entrance there, so six films each wrote a device of their own to hold it still (`hookhold`, `numhold`, `stampstand`), and the plan check read such a copy as two short stands (2026-11-26). `hook`, `number` and `stamp` take `standing: true` like the other overlays.
- **A pass that moved a drawing twitched the cuts beside it.** Three cuts in one film and one in another jumped after a first polish pass: something that stood on both sides of a cut had been moved, resized or brought in again on one side only (2026-11-26). `changed` lists the cuts next to every changed shot, and `polish.js` has its builder look at each of those frame pairs before it reports.
- **`changed` named untouched clips on every pass.** A re-encoded clip of fine moving texture reads 36 to 45 dB with nothing changed, under the 45 dB line, so every pass explained five or six clips by hand. Two more measures sort them (both pictures scaled down 16 times, and the worst block of 16 px). Neither will do alone: a grade fails only the first, and on a test film a box of 24 x 10 px read 45.05 dB scaled down, so only the block measure caught it.
- **A film's device named like a built-in type was never drawn.** The kit looks a type up among its own first (`TYPES[spec.type] || custom[spec.type]`), so a film's own `stamp` did nothing and no check said so (2026-11-26). Lint L19 refuses an overlay that asks for such a device; its list of built-in types is compared with `kit.js` in the lint's selftest.

## Process lessons

- **Fresh eyes find what self-checks miss.** Every builder reported its still-frame gates green; the reviewer still found a real visual fault in each first review. Keep the reviewer step even when the numbers pass.
- **Re-review after any change.** Two of the three late faults came from changes made after a REVIEW PASS (one: a line-height tweak grew a header and hid the top of a counted outline). List every change with its times and have a fresh reviewer check those frames and about 0.5 s either side.
- **Check holds before building.** `qa.py holds <scenario.md>` flagged 27 of the batch's 83 storyboard lines, including every one a builder later had to re-time.
- **Facts are checked in the main chat.** The builder cannot browse; put checked facts and sources into the scenario before the build.
- **Review the gates too.** In the 2.2.0 upgrade every lane's own planted faults passed, and seven fresh-eyes code reviewers still found 81 issues (13 high) with reproductions; 80 were fixed and one was rejected with its limit written down. Five calibration runs on the approved samples showed new scans raising up to 114 false pops a minute before tuning. Calibrate a new gate on the approved samples and have a reviewer reproduce its edge cases before trusting it.

## How long a video takes (5 videos of 60 s, built in parallel on 2026-10-28)

Machine: 32 logical cores, 32 GB RAM, ffmpeg 9.0.2, five builders sharing the CPU.

| Phase | Time per video |
| --- | --- |
| Scenarios (all five, with fact checks) | about 5 min in the main chat |
| Proof (engine copy, scenes.py, score, proof sheet, still gates) | 39 to 46 min of agent time, 270k to 390k tokens |
| Proof review, fix, re-review | 7 to 10 min, 3 to 6 min, 5 to 8 min |
| Render (frames, audio, master, encode, `qa.py check`) | 6.5 to 17 min per agent turn; the frames alone 14 to 150 s on 4 to 6 processes |
| Final review | 7 to 11 min |

All five went from launch to finished file in about 1 h 16 min. Writing `scenes.py` and the score is most of the time; frames are pure functions of t, so rendering is cheap. Per-frame cost ran from 3 ms (flat shapes) to about 360 ms.

A 3D card (one 16.5 s build at 24 fps, 2026-11-10, RTX 4070 Laptop): stills under a second each, the proof plate 3 min, a final plate 17 to 20 min, `run_all.py full` under a minute. The final plate was rendered four times, because each review changed the stage: budget an hour of GPU for the renders alone, and stage with stills until every scene reads before the first one.
